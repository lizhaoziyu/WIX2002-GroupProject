# EcoSwap: A Peer-to-Peer Web Application Prototype for Campus Second-Hand Item Exchange and Upcycling

**Course**: WIX2002 Project Management · **Team**: OCC2 – Team 5

## 1. Project Title
EcoSwap: A Peer-to-Peer Web Application Prototype for Campus Second-Hand Item Exchange and Upcycling

## 2. Problem Statement
University students often have unused items such as textbooks, clothing, electronic accessories and household essentials that remain in good condition. However, many students lack a convenient platform to exchange these items within their campus community. As a result, reusable items may be discarded or left unused, while other students continue purchasing similar products at full price. This issue contributes to unnecessary consumption and waste, particularly during semester transitions and student accommodation changes.

EcoSwap aims to address this problem by providing a dedicated campus platform where students can list, discover and exchange second-hand items. The project promotes responsible consumption, extends the useful life of everyday products and supports environmental sustainability.

**Campus Observation**: During a one-hour observation at the waste collection point of Southview on `20/10/2026`, the team recorded **6 discarded items** that were still in usable condition, including clothing, textbooks and small electrical appliances.

## 3. Project Objectives
The primary objective of EcoSwap is to develop a functional web application prototype within the 12-week project period to support second-hand item exchange among university students.
- Develop a responsive web application that allows students to list and browse second-hand items, with essential features implemented by Week 9.
- Provide a straightforward way for users to view item details and contact other students to arrange exchanges.
- Evaluate the usability and functionality of the prototype through testing with at least 10 student participants by Week 11.
- Facilitate at least 20 item listings and 10 completed exchanges among student testers by Week 11, encouraging reuse and upcycling over new purchases.

## 4. Project Deliverables
- **Functional Web Application Prototype**: Responsive EcoSwap platform accessible through desktop and mobile web browsers.
- **Item Listing and Browsing Features**: Interface that enables users to post items with relevant descriptions, images and categories.
- **Item Exchange Communication**: Basic mechanism for interested users to contact item owners and arrange exchanges.
- **User Guide**: Documentation explaining the platform's essential functions and usage.
- **Testing and Evaluation Report**: Summary of user testing results, identified issues and improvements.
- **Project Documentation**: Records of the development process, project progress and final outcomes.

## 6. Technical Requirements (Initial Plan)
- **Prototyping Platform**: Glide (low-code) for building the functional prototype *(Note: upgraded to Java 21 / Spring Boot 3.5 / MySQL 8 in actual implementation)*.
- **UI/UX Design**: Figma for wireframing and interface design.
- **Project Management**: Trello and Google Workspace for task tracking, communication, documentation and progress monitoring.
- **Testing Environment**: Modern desktop and mobile web browsers.

## 7. Limits and Exclusions
- No separate native Android or iOS applications.
- No online payment gateways or financial transaction processing.
- No delivery, transportation or physical item storage services.
- No advanced AI-based recommendation systems.
- No large-scale commercial deployment beyond the initial university community.

## 8. Stakeholders and Roles
- **University Students**: Primary users who list, discover and exchange second-hand items.
- **External Collaborator**: Provides input on campus second-hand item practices, reviews progress, offers feedback and signs the collaboration letter.
- **Course Lecturer**: Supervises project activities and evaluates performance.
- **Project Team**: See [team-roles-and-responsibilities.md](team-roles-and-responsibilities.md).

## 9. Reviews with Customers
- **Requirements Review (Weeks 3–4)**: Gather student opinions through surveys or interviews.
- **Design Review (Weeks 5–6)**: Present initial wireframes and user flow to the collaborator.
- **Development Review (Weeks 7–9)**: Share prototype progress and demonstrate implemented features.
- **User Acceptance Testing (Weeks 10–11)**: Invite at least 10 university students to evaluate usability and functionality.
- **Final Review (Week 12)**: Present the completed prototype and summarize testing outcomes.
